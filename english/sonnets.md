# Sonnet Collection

### Sonnet I — The Weight of Stars

Beneath the weight of stars I stand alone,
The night wraps round me like a velvet shroud,
My voice returns as echo, carved in stone,
A whispered prayer dissolving in the cloud.

The moon ascends her throne of silver light,
And casts her gaze upon the sleeping earth,
While shadows dance where none may see the sight,
And silence sings of stillness and of birth.

The rivers carry secrets to the sea,
And mountains hold the memories of rain,
The forest whispers what it means to be,
And time erases joy as much as pain.

So let the stars bear witness from above,
I find my peace in solitude and love.

### Sonnet II — Digital Garden

We plant our thoughts in rows of ones and zeros,
And tend the fields of data, line by line,
The harvest yields no fruit, produces no heroes,
Yet still we call this barren output "mine."

The cursor blinks, a heartbeat on the screen,
A pulse of light where meaning tries to grow,
Between the tags, a world remains unseen,
A garden made of logic, row by row.

But flowers cannot bloom in silicon soil,
And roots cannot take hold in shifting sand,
The beauty withers under endless toil,
As code decays beneath the coder's hand.

So let us plant in dirt and not in wire,
And grow a world that never will expire.

### Sonnet III — The Clockmaker

The clockmaker whose hands have grown too old,
To place the final gear within the wheel,
Has watched a hundred mechanisms fold,
And heard the last of every dying peal.

The cogs and springs that once obeyed his will,
Now rust upon the shelves of time forgot,
The pendulum hangs heavy, cold and still,
The echoes fade where once the chimes were wrought.

Yet in the silence something still remains,
A memory of order in the void,
The patience born of measuring the chains,
That bind the universe, neither destroyed.

For every clock that stops, another starts,
And time rolls on through hands and ticking hearts.

### Sonnet IV — Winter Commute

The platform fills with faces cold and gray,
As winter breathes its frost upon the train,
We wait in silence, nothing left to say,
Our footsteps lost in ice and freezing rain.

The carriage groans and shudders into flight,
While coffee cups grow cold against the skin,
The windows blur with breath and fading light,
And somewhere past the glass, the dusk begins.

But in this cage of steel and weary bone,
A stranger shares a smile across the aisle,
A moment brief, yet somehow not alone,
A spark of warmth against the winter's guile.

For even in the coldest rush of days,
A nod, a glance, can set the soul ablaze.
